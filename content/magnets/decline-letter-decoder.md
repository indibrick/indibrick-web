## Why banks decline

Every Canadian A-lender operates under **OSFI Guideline B-20**, a set of federal underwriting rules that dictate which files they can and cannot fund. When a branch tells you "you don't qualify," what they're really saying is: *your file doesn't fit our institution's B-20 interpretation.* Another lender — with a different interpretation, appetite, or program — may fund exactly the same file tomorrow.

This decoder maps the top 6 reasons Canadian banks decline mortgages against what a broker actually does with the file next.

## The 6 real reasons banks say no

### 1. "Insufficient income" or "TDS/GDS ratios too high"

**What it usually means:** your monthly debt payments (including the new mortgage at the stress-tested qualifying rate) exceed 44% of your gross income.

**What a broker looks at:**
- Alt-A lenders that use bank-statement income (not T4)
- B-lenders that go to 50%+ TDS with compensating factors
- Extended amortization (30 or 35 years) at B-lender level
- Stated-income products for self-employed files

**Typical placement:** Alt-A or B-lender at 5.99–7.99% with no fee to you if the rate is buy-down eligible.

### 2. "Bruised credit"

**What it usually means:** credit score under 660, recent late payments, or a consumer proposal on file.

**What a broker looks at:**
- Alt-A programs that accept 580+ with compensating factors
- B-lenders that specialize in 500+ files
- 6-month credit rebuild path before A-lender re-submission
- Private lender bridge if timeline is urgent (with documented exit)

**Typical placement:** B-lender at 6.99–8.99% with a 6–12 month exit plan back to A-lender pricing.

### 3. "Self-employed / income not verifiable"

**What it usually means:** your bank uses net income after write-offs, which is far below your actual cash flow.

**What a broker looks at:**
- Alt-A programs that gross-up self-employed income
- B-lenders that use bank-statement income (12 months of deposits)
- Stated-income products (up to 65% LTV, no T1 required)
- Add-backs for write-offs the bank refused to consider

**Typical placement:** Alt-A at A-lender rate + 25–50 bps, no fee.

### 4. "New to Canada / no credit history"

**What it usually means:** your bank's newcomer program is narrower than they publicly advertise.

**What a broker looks at:**
- Big 6 newcomer programs (each has different underwriting)
- Foreign credit report acceptance (Equifax International, Nova Credit)
- 12 months rental history as credit proxy
- ITIN-only lending path at two B-lenders

**Typical placement:** A-lender newcomer program at standard pricing, sometimes with 10% down instead of 5%.

### 5. "Property issue" (appraisal / marketability)

**What it usually means:** appraisal came in low, property is rural/unique, or building has known issues.

**What a broker looks at:**
- Second appraisal by a different appraiser (sometimes the first was wrong)
- Alt-A lenders with broader property acceptance
- Bridge financing to close on time while a solution is built
- Credit union options for non-standard properties

**Typical placement:** credit union or alt-A depending on the specific property issue.

### 6. "System decline" (no specific reason given)

**What it usually means:** your bank's automated underwriting flagged something the branch rep couldn't override.

**What a broker looks at:**
- Different lenders use different automated systems — a decline at one is not a decline at another
- Manual underwriting at credit unions
- File repositioning to address the specific automated flag

**Typical placement:** A-lender at another institution, often with the same rate.

## What to send us

To review your file properly, we need:

1. **The decline letter itself** — even a screenshot works
2. **The rejection reason the bank gave** (verbal is fine, we'll interpret)
3. **The purpose of the mortgage** (purchase / refinance / renewal)
4. **Rough numbers**: household income, credit score range, down payment %

We'll come back within **24 hours** with a lender-tier plan: which A-lenders will approve as-is, which B-lender rescues, and if private is needed, the exit strategy back to A pricing.
